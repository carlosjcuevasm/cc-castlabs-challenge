    • Establishing Context
      • The Challenge Sent
        • Solution design
          • We would like you to work on a customer issue and propose a solution during our next meeting.
          • Please take a look at our DRMtoday product here: https://castlabs.com/security/drmtoday/
          • And see how it works at the high level https://castlabs.com/security/drmtoday/how-it-works/
          • DRMtoday supports key ingestion over HTTPS using an authenticated protocol (via CAS - Central Authentication Service) and can either generate or accept keys from the ingest request.
          • The customer issue is the following:
            • Our client is a broadcaster company who has recently launched a VoD service on OTT and is facing some end-user errors (DRM license request timeout) when a new famous asset is published. They would like to improve their DRM licensing infrastructure which is an on-premise server where they save all the content keys and deliver the DRM licenses to devices. They have access to their content keys with a simple REST request.
            • A migration to a new server infrastructure is planned but the service continuity is super important.
          • During our meeting you will explain a possible solution using DRMtoday and its advantages. You will propose a simple migration plan with a rough timeline.
      • Current state
        • Who: A broadcaster.
        • Business context: Recently launched an OTT video-on-demand (VOD) service
        • Observed Problem: When a popular new asset is published, some end users experience DRM license-request timeouts.
        • When it happens:
          • At launch of a popular asset.
          • Thus, likely, during a traffic spike.
        • Why it matters:
          • A DRM license request is within the critical path playback. Even if the video is available through the CDN and the player can download its segments, playback won't work as expected until the device obtain a valid license in time.
        • Business Impact: Potential playback failures and degraded user experience during high-demand launches.
          • Affected parties
            • End users attempting to play popular VOD assets when license requests time out.
            • The broadcaster and stakeholders, through potential customer dissatisfaction, potential support burden, and reduced service reliability.
          • Severity / urgency: High due to potential reputational damage. A high-profile launch with problems creates a poor impression on users and reputation degrades
      • Desired Outcome
        • Must be attained (Intentionally unquantified requirements)
          • DRM license-request timeouts do not happen on popular launches.
          • High license-request success rate and acceptable response latency.
          • Migration with minimal service disruption.
      • Solution Reasoning Framework
        • Facts, Assumptions and Contingency
          • There is not additional information about the customer operation modules and associated architecture. I feel most comfortable by distinguishing the types of information I'll be handling and building on.
          • Thus... I'm to establish
            • Facts: Things explicitly stated about the customer operations and the observed problem.
            • Unknowns: Information that I cannot determine from the available evidence.
            • Reasonable Assumptions: Inferences from usual industry practices and the known facts. They're kind of expected.
            • Working Assumptions: Just decisions made to proceed with the solution design, despite missing information.
            • Contingency: Preliminary plan or course of action to take when something is not possible as presumed
        • DRM System Model
          • Reasonable Assumption
            • I'll be working around the understanding of the usual DRM system architecture, scoped for this solution design. Because I cannot know more about the customer, this also serve as the basis of it's reasoning, and would be valid.
              • The Packager: Prepares the content for delivery. It requests (or informs) of a Content Key (secret symmetrical encryption key) to the DRM system (Key Management).
              • Key management: Generates, stores, manages, or supplies content encryption keys and their associated identifiers.
              • Entitlement and authorization: The broadcaster determines whether the user is permitted to access the content or not, and communicates authorization to the license server.
              • License server: Validates a license request and returns a license constrained by the DRM policy in place.
              • Player / User: The player / device requests a license; It decrypts as expected once it receives it. Playback happens as expected if the others are working fine.
            • License serving is a cross-cutting concern, these components follow paths of communication among them to reach a smooth playback of decrypted content.
    • Issue Assessment and Scope
      • Fact: The broadcaster experiences DRM license-request timeouts when a popular VOD asset launches.
      • Unknow: The precise cause of timeouts.
        • The issue is observed in the DRM licensing workflow. That in itself does not mean, it's also the culprit.
        • Other components or dependencies in the licensing workflow could contribute to the failure. In a real scenario, to solve this issue truly, this would need to be at least assessed.
          • This is so even knowing they handle the licensing workflow with on-premise infrastructure, an easy suspect of scalability issues.
          • Maybe an incorrect key mapping on "launch titles" and configuration issues, maybe Entitlement servers is faulty etc.
      • Working assumption: They issue is isolated to the licensing infrastructure
        • This won't be a consultancy on their infrastructure, to close scope, this would be true.
        • The underlying problem may involve peak-load capacity or an implementation issue within the existing licensing infrastructure
    • Solution Design
      • Customer features
        • Fact: The customer existing on-premises server stores content keys and delivers DRM licenses.
        • Fact: The customer access key provision with a simple HTTPS REST request
        • Unknown: Whether the customer's existing HTTPS REST API supports key provision in a format compatible with DRMtoday's ingestion workflow.
          • Working assumption: Existing keys can be securely exported and ingested, either directly or through a small integration component.
        • Unknown: If the customer delivery infrastructure allows for a gradual routing license requests to another service scheme.
          • Working assumption: The customer infrastructure allows for progressive rollout
      • DRMToday features
        • Fact: DRMtoday supports multiple key-ingestion methods, including integrations such as AWS SPEKE, CPIX, and Harmonic KMS.
        • Fact: DRMToday stores content keys and associated metadata to it's secure database for future license delivery
        • Fact: DRMToday supports a managed cloud service and also a self-hosted option
        • Fact: DRMToday infrastructure is very scalable, it grows to meet demand.
        • Fact: DRMToday supports token authorization so the customer checks authorization first and gives DRMToday a secure token it validates during the license request
        • Fact: DRMtoday supports individual license-request details, delivery logs, reporting APIs, and log integration with data pipelines.
        • Fact: DRMtoday offers a globally distributed, multi-region licensing service.
      • Solution
        • The target can reasonably be a documented DRMtoday integration pattern
        • 			  Existing key API
			         ↓
			  Secure key ingestion
			         ↓
			  DRMToday's secure database
			         ↓
			  DRMtoday's licensing service
			         ↓
			  User's Device
          • This results in a standard DRMtoday integration
        • Explicit Decisions
          • We are to handle secure key management and licensing as part of our DRM licensing workflow.
          • We're to use the token authorization, it helps to isolate a bit the entitlement check and possible bottlenecks on their on-premise infrastructure
          • We're to push for a managed cloud service, is preferred because it directly addresses the capacity and resilience risk at peak-load patterns seen.
          • Were to provide detailed logs about any licensing issue further down the line as we have great observability
    • Migration Plan
      • 1. Discovery and baseline
        • Confirm and establish the proposed migration is technically feasible, and document current state
        • Tasks
          • Determine whether DRMtoday can ingest the customer's existing keys directly or requires a custom integration to automate key transfer.
            • Contingency: Leverage DRMtoday's key-generation capabilities, with a custom workaround for migrating existing keys if needed
          • Confirm DRMtoday's supported key-ingestion method to use, and also compatibility with existing content and players.
          • Confirm DRM settings capabilities required are met
          • Establish current license-request success rate, timeout rate, latency, and peak traffic.
          • Confirm the customer's delivery infrastructure allows for directing license requests to the another service selectively or progressively.
            • Contigency: If progressive traffic routing is unsupported, evaluate migrating license requests for selected assets to DRMtoday, potentially by updating embedded DRM signaling. A coordinated cutover remains the fallback, with a tested rollback procedure.
        • Output:
          • Brief documented confirmed migration requirements.
          • Current state information like baseline metrics
        • Time estimate
          • 2-4 days
      • 2. Integration and configuration
        • Connect the new system in place and configure it accordingly
        • Tasks
          • Connect the existing key source to DRMtoday
            • Implement the agreed ingestion method
            • Provision the required content keys and associated metadata securely.
          • Configure the new licensing workflow
            • Configure token authorization with customer's API
            • Configure license policies
            • Setup required DRM settings
          • Devise a tested rollback option as fallback for migration safety
        • Output:
          • A DRMToday instance is configured, has the required key material and metadata, along with correct licensing workflow.
      • 3. Functional and load testing
        • Make sure the new workflow works as expected, on relevant scenarios, in a controlled environment.
        • Tasks (not exahustive)
          • Check key-to-asset matching, supported DRM systems, and relevant player/device combinations
          • Verify that authorized users can obtain licenses and play assets
          • Test expired or invalid tokens and unauthorized requests
          • Simulate expected peak load and bursts. Measure latency, timeout rate, and failures.
        • Output:
          • Evidence that playback, authorization, security, performance and overall functioning meet agreed acceptance criteria
      • 4.Pilot and limited rollout
        • Introduce the new licensing path to a small, controlled portion of the service.
        • Tasks
          • Start with selected compatible assets or a controlled user cohort scheme.
          • Monitor real license requests and playback outcomes.
          • Compare results with the baseline and check for unexpected failures.
        • Output:
          • Production evidence that the new path works under real conditions before wider adoption.
      • 5. Progressive rollout and monitoring
        • Goal is a gradual adoption of the tested solution. Increase traffic in stages rather than switching everything at once without backup plan.
        • Tasks
          • Expand the rollout as each stage meets the agreed criteria.
          • Monitor license success, timeout rate, latency, traffic, and provider errors.
          • Define who can pause or reverse the rollout and what thresholds trigger the decision.
        • Output:
          • The new system handles the intended production traffic with measurable stability.
      • 6. Stabilization and decommissioning
        • Keep the old system available until the new one is proven stable.
        • Tasks
          • Maintain the rollback option for an agreed observation period.
          • Confirm operational ownership, alerting, support procedures, and incident escalation arragements.
          • Retire the old licensing service, but only after dependencies are removed and sign-off is complete.
        • Output:
          • Approved new service and a safely retired old path, if retirement is appropriate.

        • Estimates are rough planning placeholders. I don't know the customer's existing tooling, how much DRMtoday-specific work is required, or the process of familiarity with DRMToday.

          Discovery and baseline 2–5 days
          Integration and configuration 3–10 days

        • DRM expertise and 24/7 expert support, depending on the selected service plan.
